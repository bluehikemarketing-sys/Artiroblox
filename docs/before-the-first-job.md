# Before the first job: New York checklist, and New York vs New Jersey

Researched October 2026. This is a business checklist, not legal advice: have a New York accountant confirm the entity and tax steps once, and an attorney review the service agreement once. Items marked **(verify)** came from secondary sources that disagreed or couldn't be confirmed against the official page.

## The short answer

**Start in New York, Nassau County first.** New Jersey saves roughly $1,000 in setup, but the toll back into New York costs about $15 a night and wipes out the saving within a few months. New Jersey also has no advantage on demand, and everything you've built (the site, the Google profile under review, the 516 number) points at New York. Make New Jersey a later expansion once you have a crew.

---

## Part 1: everything to have in place before the first job (New York)

"Required" means the law requires it. "Expected" means clients and buildings will ask for it before they let you in.

### A. The business entity

| # | Item | Cost | Required? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Florida LLC in good standing | $138.75/yr annual report, due May 1 (verify) | Yes, to keep the LLC | Check sunbiz.org. A missed report means a $400 late fee and eventually dissolution. Florida also requires a registered agent with a Florida street address. |
| 2 | New York Application for Authority (register the Florida LLC to do business in NY) | $250 (verify) | Yes | File with the NY Department of State before you invoice anyone in NY. |
| 3 | Publication: run a notice for 6 weeks in two newspapers the county clerk picks, then file a Certificate of Publication | Newspapers ~$1,000–1,500+ in NYC counties (vendor estimate), less elsewhere; certificate $50 | Yes, within 120 days of #2 | If you miss it, your authority to do business is suspended, and an LLC without authority can't sue in NY courts. That means you can't take a client to court for an unpaid invoice. The county is the one where your NY office is, so it can't be picked just because it's cheaper. |
| 4 | Certificate of Assumed Name for "Clean by Morning" | $25 state fee; whether NYC county fees also apply to LLCs is unclear (verify) | Yes, if the LLC's legal name isn't "Clean by Morning" | **Important:** the website currently gives the legal name as "Clean by Morning Office Cleaning". It must be the LLC's real registered name, with "Clean by Morning" as the assumed name. Send me the exact LLC name and I'll fix the site. |
| 5 | EIN and a business bank account | Free | EIN yes; account strongly advised | Keep business money separate, or the LLC's liability protection is easy to attack. |
| 6 | NY LLC filing fee (Form IT-204-LL) and the biennial statement | $25 minimum a year; $9 every two years | Yes | Small, but missed filings add up. |

**Alternative to #1–3:** dissolve the Florida LLC and form a New York LLC ($200 plus the same publication). That gets rid of the Florida fees and registered agent. Ask the accountant which is cheaper over 3 years. Either way, publication is unavoidable in New York.

### B. Taxes

| # | Item | Cost | Required? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | NY sales tax Certificate of Authority | Free | **Yes, at least 20 days before your first sale** | NY taxes interior cleaning and janitorial services whatever the contract length (confirmed in the state's own bulletin TSB-M-91(4)S, and in the 2012 notice that combined the 30-day lines). ### C. Workers and helpers (this is where NY fines are biggest)

| # | Item | Cost | Required? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | Workers' comp exemption certificate CE-200 | Free | Expected | With no employees you don't need workers' comp, disability or paid family leave insurance. The CE-200 proves that to clients and buildings that ask. |
| 12 | **The day you bring anyone with you**, even a friend for one night, even part-time | Workers' comp + disability + paid family leave policies; payroll | **Yes, before they work** | NY presumes anyone you pay to clean is an employee, not a 1099 contractor. Working without coverage brings fines from the Workers' Compensation Board for every period you're uncovered, plus personal liability if they get hurt. ### E. New York City traps

- **Trash:** take bags only to the spot where the client's building or hauler collects them. Never carry a client's trash away in your car. In NYC, removing a business's waste is trade-waste hauling and needs a Business Integrity Commission license.
- **Manhattan congestion toll:** $9 with E-ZPass if you enter below 60th Street from 5 AM to 9 PM on weekdays, or $2.25 overnight. You're charged once a day. Include it in any Manhattan quote.
- **Parking:** park legally, or in a garage. One ticket can wipe out a night's profit. Don't stop in commercial-only or loading zones with passenger plates, and don't double-park while unloading.
- **Buildings:** before the first night, register as a vendor with the building, get the certificate of insurance approved, and confirm service hours and freight elevator access.
- **Medical offices:** a janitorial contractor generally isn't a HIPAA "business associate" and doesn't need a business associate agreement (HHS FAQ 243), as long as you never handle records. Offer a short confidentiality agreement anyway, since practices like it. Never touch sharps or regulated medical waste. Get the hepatitis B vaccine yourself.
- **Chemicals:** use EPA-registered disinfectants exactly as the label says, including contact time. Under federal law the label is the law. Keep a safety data sheet for every product, and label any bottle you refill. That becomes an OSHA requirement once you have employees.
- **Cold email (CAN-SPAM):**
  - Every email needs a real postal address. Use a PO box or virtual mailbox, not your home.
  - Include a working unsubscribe and honor it within 10 business days.
  - No misleading subject lines.
  - Don't cold-text people. Texting has much heavier penalties under the federal TCPA.

## Part 2: New York vs New Jersey, side by side

Assumes you live in NYC, work alone, and drive.

| | **New York (Nassau first, then Brooklyn, Manhattan selectively)** | **New Jersey (Bergen / Hudson)** |
|---|---|---|
| Register the Florida LLC | $250 (verify) | $125 (verify), plus a NJ registered agent (you have no NJ address), ~$50–150/yr |
| Publication | Yes, ~$550–1,600 | None |
| Yearly state upkeep | IT-204-LL $25+, biennial $9 | Annual report $75 |
| Sales tax on cleaning | 8.875% NYC / 8.625% Nassau | 6.625% (janitorial reported taxable; verify on nj.gov) |
| Minimum wage once you hire | $17.00 | $15.92 |
| Local business taxes | NYC UBT over ~$95k gross; MCTMT over $50k net | None comparable, but you'd file a NJ nonresident return and still pay NY and NYC resident tax on everything, since you live in NYC |
| Daily travel cost | Nassau: none. Manhattan: $2.25–9 congestion toll | **George Washington Bridge back into NY: ~$14.75 off-peak, $16.79 peak with E-ZPass** (verify). At 5 nights a week that's ~$3,800/yr |
| Workers' comp with no employees | Exempt (CE-200) | Exempt (LLC members excluded) |
| Demand | Dense medical and dental, in Nassau's office parks and Brooklyn's ground-floor practices | Also dense (Bergen County medical buildings) |
| Logistics | Nassau: parking lots, easy nights. Manhattan: buildings, COIs, parking | Suburban parking, but every night includes a bridge |
| Fit with what's already built | Site, Google profile under review, 516 number, area pages | Would mean changing the Google service area mid-review, which can restart it |

**First-year cost to work in New Jersey:** about $1,000 less to set up, then about $3,800 more a year in tolls, plus a second state tax return. It costs more by month 3–4.

**Opportunity:** roughly equal per mile driven. New York has the bigger total market and the highest prices (Manhattan). Nassau gives you New Jersey's easy suburban logistics without the bridge.

### Recommendation

1. **Now:** New York, starting with Nassau County (Garden City, Mineola, Great Neck, Lake Success medical buildings), then Brooklyn. Take Manhattan jobs only when they're priced for the toll, parking and building paperwork.
2. **Later:** add New Jersey when a hired crew can work there without you driving the bridge every night. Register then: it takes $125 and a few days, with no publication.
3. **Don't change your Google profile's service areas** while it's being verified.
