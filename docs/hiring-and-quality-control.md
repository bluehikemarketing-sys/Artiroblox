# Hiring and quality control

The rule: **you control the business through systems, not by watching people.** If every site has a written checklist, photo proof, a clock-in at the address and a regular inspection, a bad hire shows up within a week instead of when a client cancels.

## 1. Before the first hire

- **Clean every new account yourself for its first 2–4 weeks.** You learn the site, write its checklist and set the standard the new person is measured against.
- **Hire one person before you need them.** Train them as your backup while you still do the work, so the first no-show doesn't cost you a client.
- **Write the site binder as you go.** For each site, keep:
  - the checklist, room by room
  - photos of what "done" looks like
  - access steps (entrance, alarm, lights)
  - the supply closet location
  - client do's and don'ts

## 2. Who to hire

**Hire for reliability over experience.** Cleaning can be taught in three shifts. Showing up at 6 PM every night can't be taught.

| Step | What happens | What you're testing |
|---|---|---|
| Job post | Indeed or Craigslist, plus referrals from people you trust. Pay range in the post (NYC law requires it) | — |
| 10-minute phone screen | Availability, transportation, which nights they can commit to | Do they answer and call back when they say they will? |
| **Paid trial shift** alongside you (NY requires paying for it) | One real clean at one of your sites | Pace, attention to detail, phone habits, how they take correction |
| Conditional offer | In writing, at a fixed rate | — |
| Background check, after the offer only | NYC's Fair Chance Act says criminal history comes after a conditional offer, and NY requires an individual assessment before you withdraw it | Theft or violence relevant to holding keys to medical offices |
| References | Two calls, ask only "would you rehire them?" | — |

**Pay above minimum.** The legal minimum is $17/hr. Pay **$20–22/hr** plus a quality bonus. Night cleaners leave over a dollar or two, and replacing one costs more than the raise. At your rate model, labor still runs well under half of revenue.

**Immediate no:** late to the trial shift, on their phone in the client's space, opening drawers or doors that weren't part of the job, arguing with correction.

## 3. Onboarding (first two weeks)

1. **Two shadow shifts** at each site they'll own, then one shift where they lead and you inspect.
2. **Training on:**
   - color-coded cloths, so restroom cloths never touch exam rooms
   - disinfectant contact times
   - the never list
3. **Sign the house rules:**
   - **Never** (immediate termination): no guests, no one else on site, nothing taken, no opening drawers, files or screens, no use of client phones, computers, food or drinks.
   - **Always:** clock in at the site, follow the checklist, take end-of-clean photos, set the alarm, text the lead if anything is wrong.
4. **90-day probation**, with a check-in at day 30 and day 60.

## 4. Control systems (these do the supervising)

| System | How | What it catches |
|---|---|---|
| Clock-in at the address | App with a location fence. Clock-in only works at the client's address | No-shows, late starts, short shifts |
| Checklist in the app | Each room is a task, checked off on site | Skipped rooms |
| End-of-clean photos | 4–6 photos per visit, same angles every time, timestamped | Rushed work, and it doubles as your proof to the client |
| Inspections | You or a crew lead score each site: weekly in month 1, then every 2 weeks, then monthly | Slow slips in quality |
| Client check-in | One text a month: "Anything we missed?" | Problems before they turn into cancellations |
| Key control | Keys on numbered tags with no addresses; a sign-out log; a personal alarm code per cleaner where the system allows | Lost keys, and who was in the building |
| Same person per site | Each site has an owner and a trained backup | Accountability: there's no "someone else did it" |

Software that does the first three in one app: **Swept** (built for janitorial companies) or **Connecteam** (cheaper, general purpose). Under NY law you must tell employees in writing about any electronic monitoring, and get their acknowledgment, before you use it. Put it in the house rules they sign.

**The scorecard (inspections):** score each room 1–3. A site averaging under 2.5 gets a re-clean that night and a coaching conversation. Two low scores in 30 days is a final warning.

## 5. Scaling the control

| Stage | Structure | Your job |
|---|---|---|
| 1–8 sites | You + 1–2 cleaners | Inspect everything, clean when needed |
| 8–20 sites | 2 crews of 2 + **1 crew lead** (+$2–3/hr) who cleans and inspects | Hire, sell, inspect a sample of sites |
| 20–40 sites | 4 crews + 2 crew leads + **1 operations lead** | Sales, numbers, the operations lead's performance |
| 40+ sites | Add 1 crew lead per ~10 sites and a backup cleaner per ~5 sites | The business, not the night shift |

**What keeps quality from falling apart at scale:**
1. **A bench.** Keep one trained backup for every ~5 sites. No-shows kill night cleaning companies, not bad mopping.
2. **Promote from within.** Your best cleaner from month 3 is your crew lead in month 5. They know your standard, and it shows the team a path up.
3. **Pay for quality, not hours.** A monthly bonus ($50–100) for cleaners whose sites average 2.8+ on inspections with zero client complaints. Crew leads get a bonus tied to client retention.
4. **Fire fast, but by the rules.** Coach once, warn in writing, then let them go. Every warning and score lives in the app, so a decision never rests on memory.
5. **Look at the numbers weekly:**
   - on-time clock-ins (target 98%)
   - average inspection score
   - client complaints
   - cleaner turnover

## 6. New York legal guardrails for hiring

- Pay helpers as W-2 employees, not 1099 contractors. NY presumes cleaners are employees.
- Workers' comp, disability and paid family leave insurance must be in place before the first shift.
- I-9 for every hire. Written pay notice at hire.
- Post the pay range in NYC job ads.
- Ask about criminal history only after a conditional offer (NYC Fair Chance Act).
- **No credit checks in NYC.**
- **Don't test for cannabis.** NY protects lawful off-duty use. You can still act on impairment at work.
- Pay for trial shifts and training.
- Give written notice of electronic monitoring and get it acknowledged.
- NYC sick leave applies from the first employee.
